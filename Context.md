2. Event Concept and Positioning
HackO'Ween brings together technology, creativity, and a
Halloween-inspired atmosphere. Participants collaborate to turn ideas
into functional prototypes within a limited time. The focus is on
practical problem-solving, thoughtful technical execution, and solutions
that can create meaningful impact---not just a visually polished
presentation.

Suggested positioning line:
Build through the night. Bring bold ideas to life with AI.

The event is intended to encourage experimentation, teamwork, rapid
prototyping, and entrepreneurial thinking in a high-energy hackathon
environment.
3. Hackathon Themes
Theme A --- AI After Dark: Agentic AI
Projects may explore intelligent systems, autonomous or semi-autonomous
agents, automation tools, AI-powered workflows, and next-generation AI
products.

Possible directions (illustrative, not official problem statements): -
AI agents that complete useful multi-step workflows - Productivity and
research assistants - Intelligent automation for real-world tasks -
Multi-agent or tool-using systems - AI solutions that improve
accessibility or service delivery
Theme B --- SDG and Social Impact
Projects should align with at least one of the 17 United Nations
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and address a real social,
environmental, or economic challenge.

Possible directions (illustrative, not official problem statements): -
Education and digital inclusion - Health and well-being - Climate action
and sustainability - Clean water, sanitation, or energy - Sustainable
cities and communities - Responsible consumption - Employment,
livelihoods, and inclusion

Important: These examples are brainstorming directions only. Do not
present them as official, pre-announced problem statements.

7. Judging and Evaluation
Round 1 video-shortlisting criteria (as stated in the listing)
1. Innovation
2. Feasibility
3. Clarity of presentation
4. Technical approach
5. Potential impact
Suggested framework for the offline round
The event description confirms that final project demonstrations and
presentations will be evaluated by judges, but it does not specify a
complete official scoring rubric. The following is a proposed
organizer framework, not an official published rubric:
- Problem understanding and relevance: Is the problem clearly
  identified and does the solution address it?
- Innovation and originality: Is the approach meaningfully
  differentiated?
- Technical implementation: Does the prototype work, and are the
  technical choices appropriate?
- Use of AI / agentic approach (where relevant): Is AI used
  purposefully rather than added as decoration?
- Impact and feasibility: Could the solution realistically help
  its intended users?
- Demo and communication: Can the team explain the workflow,
  decisions, limitations, and results clearly?
For fairness, publish the final scoring weights and any theme-specific
requirements to all teams before judging begins. If problem statements
are revealed on the spot, ensure teams are evaluated against the same
announced criteria and the problem statement assigned to them.
